# Campaign design

Use this reference to choose messages, order, and exact copy. Establish the product evidence before choosing a visual metaphor.

## Match the audience's decision

Identify the concrete job the app helps someone complete and the uncertainty that might stop them trying it. The opening should make the category and benefit understandable to a newcomer. Subsequent panels should answer different questions, such as whether the app handles the user's real task, how much effort it takes, or why it is useful repeatedly.

An illustrative sequence is promise → core workflow → useful depth → another supported outcome → repeat value. Adapt it to the app and available evidence. A single-purpose app may need fewer panels; a detailed product may need more. Do not pad the set with repeated feature lists or unsupported claims.

Use the strongest material early. Do not depend on a viewer seeing the whole sequence or on a specific number of panels being visible in search. Confirm current placement behavior when it matters to the brief.

## Build a compact storyboard

Use one row per planned panel. Keep IDs stable across revisions, independent of export order.

| Field | Decision it records |
| --- | --- |
| Panel ID and order | Which artwork this is and where it appears |
| Audience question | What hesitation or need it addresses |
| Benefit | One supported result the viewer should understand |
| Exact headline | Approved or proposed wording with deliberate line breaks |
| Optional support | Extra detail only when the headline and visual cannot carry it |
| Proof | Original capture/photo and relevant visible feature or state |
| Visual idea | Composition, crop, and the role of any person or illustration |
| Neighbor link | A planned seam or continuity relationship, if any |
| Open issue | Missing evidence, uncertain wording, or production dependency |

User-approved copy remains fixed unless edits were requested. New copy can be selected using judgment within the brief; it does not require a separate approval round by default.

## Turn principles into choices

| Principle | Useful question | Correction when it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clear value | Can a new viewer tell why this app matters? | Replace a vague slogan with a supported outcome. |
| Outcomes with proof | Does the screen show the mechanism behind the promise? | Choose a stronger capture or narrow the claim. |
| One message | Are two benefits competing for first attention? | Pick one; move the other only if it earns another panel. |
| Obvious visual idea | Without the headline, is the use case recognizable? | Enlarge the relevant state or remove unrelated decoration. |
| Human relevance | Does a person or situation explain the benefit? | Omit lifestyle imagery that only fills space. |
| Credible trust | Is every proof point attributable and current? | Use authentic product evidence rather than inventing social proof. |
| Simple copy | Can the text be understood at listing size? | Remove words before reducing type size. |

## Write copy that can survive scrutiny

Prefer short, concrete headlines, often two to seven words, when the language and message permit it. This is a design heuristic, not a word-count requirement. Avoid splitting a meaningful phrase across lines merely to fit a decorative grid. Keep supporting copy optional.

Replace generic claims such as “Unlock your potential” with what the app enables. Do not promise speed, reliability, revenue, health outcomes, privacy guarantees, or compatibility without evidence. Keep qualifications that change a claim's meaning legible. A visible paid feature must not be represented as universally free.

For example, when a capture shows a single weekly schedule, “See your week at a glance” has visible support. “Never miss a deadline again” asserts an outcome that the capture cannot establish. This is an illustrative copy comparison, not a feature to add to unrelated campaigns.

## Localization and optimization

Translate intent before fitting lines. Keep locale-specific UI, units, prices, and examples consistent with the marketed experience. When a qualified reviewer or matching capture is unavailable, disclose the exact uncertainty; do not imply language approval.

For an optimization request, state the hypothesis and the principal variable: opening benefit, proof image, copy, or sequence. Keep unrelated variables stable when practical. A wholly different direction can be compared as a package, but its results cannot isolate which element helped. Use Apple's current [product page optimization guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/) for experiment setup; do not invent traffic, measured lift, or a statistical stopping rule.
