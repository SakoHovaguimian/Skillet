# Plan a Launch

## Outcome

Deliver one campaign plan that shows who should act, why the launch matters to them, what to produce, and when to release it. Connect the launch message and visual direction to marketing and social deliverables, then define how the team will measure the result.

## Inputs and preconditions

Use the available product brief, launch stage, audience evidence, dates, brand assets, channels, and constraints on budget, staffing, or rollout. Check which capabilities will actually be available by audience, region, platform, and date before building claims around them. Reuse existing positioning and brand decisions. When evidence is thin, label the proposed segment or message as a hypothesis.

Ask only for missing information that materially changes the plan. With a flexible date or uncertain rollout, use a relative timeline and name the condition that fixes launch day. If budget or staff is undecided, produce a viable baseline using available channels and mark additions that depend on those decisions.

## Workflow

1. Define the launch goal, primary audience, desired action, and release scope. Scale the plan to the change and the team's capacity. Name the audience that can act on day one.
2. Build the campaign around one promise and the evidence for it. Record the message, proof, voice, and visual cues supplied by the brand. Pair each asset with the job it does. If a brand guide exists, point to the relevant assets and rules. Otherwise describe a provisional campaign direction with the assumptions marked.
3. Choose channels by audience access, format, cost, and available capacity. Plan pre-launch, launch, and follow-up together. For each selected social platform, specify the account or publisher, audience, post angle, format, visuals, timing, call to action, and who handles replies. Coordinate social posts with the landing page, email, store listing, partnerships, or paid campaigns where those channels have a role.
4. Sequence the asset and publishing work. Identify owners where established, upstream dependencies, and the conditions that would delay or scale back the launch. Check that links, tracking, and destinations can be ready before the posts that depend on them. Keep ready-to-use copy or creative briefs in scope when requested.
5. Define the primary success measure, source, baseline, and review date. Add diagnostic measures only where they can change a decision. Identify who reviews early results and what would trigger a correction, follow-up, or pause. Reconcile public claims and dates across every planned channel.

## Constraints

- Ground public claims in product evidence. Mark proposed copy, unconfirmed dates, and inferred targets clearly.
- Match channels, assets, and cadence to the team's actual capacity. Assign decision owners and budgets only where supported.
- A launch plan authorizes planning and drafting. Publish, schedule, spend, or contact others only when the user authorizes those actions.

## Failure handling

- Readiness evidence is missing or conflicts with the announcement. Produce a conditional plan and identify the evidence needed before the affected copy or channel goes live.
- A planned channel or asset is unavailable. Identify the impact on audience reach and propose an available substitute or a reduced launch.
- A metric lacks a baseline or reliable tracking. Record what must be observed or instrumented before comparing results.

## Output contract

Deliver the plan in the requested medium using the [launch plan format](references/launch-plan-format.md). Use calendar dates when known and relative timing otherwise. Keep unresolved decisions and readiness conditions visible.
